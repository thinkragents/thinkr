TKR‑001 · Rev A

Evidence that existed
before the argument.

Agents pay each other and nothing records what was delivered, except what the seller says afterwards. THINKR has the seller seal every hour of deliveries on chain, before anyone could dispute them, so the record provably existed first. A bond puts money behind that record.

Live: sealing, verifying, bonds and claims. The bond contract is unaudited, so each bond is capped at $250. It replaces an earlier contract that could be claimed against unfairly; no bond was ever posted to that one.

The shape Epochs Claims Cover What it cannot do

Not escrow

The buyer's payment is never held or delayed. It passes through the contract straight to the seller in the same transaction. What sits in the contract is the seller's own money, posted in advance, covering everything it sells.

A plumber is bonded. You do not put his fee in escrow. You check his bond before you let him in the house.

The hour is the unit

Every hour a seller seals one merkle root of what it delivered in that hour. It has six more hours to seal it. After that the root is final, sealed or not.

  1. 00:00–01:00

    the seller delivers. Each delivery is a leaf: keccak256(ref, digest). The ref is what both sides call the transaction; the digest is the seller's evidence in whatever format it already keeps.

  2. 01:00–07:00

    the window to seal that hour's root. The owner, or a sealing key it appointed, seals; the owner may replace its sealing key's root inside the window, never the reverse.

  3. from 07:00

    the root is fixed. Claims on a payment open only once both hours its delivery could be sealed in are fixed, so a seller can never see a claim and then write the record that answers it.

That ordering is the whole design. Everything else is bookkeeping around it.

Silence pays out

A buyer pays a bonded seller through THINKR. The money goes straight to the seller, and the same amount of the seller's bond is reserved for that payment, so no one else can claim the cover it relies on. Only payments made this way can be claimed, only by the address that paid, and only for what was paid.

If the seller seals no record for the payment, in its hour or the next, the buyer claims and stakes 2%. The seller, or anyone holding the proof, has 48 hours to answer with the sealed record; the stake then goes to the seller. No answer, and anyone can settle: the buyer collects the payment and its stake back, out of the bond.

Cover is reserved per payment

A payment can only be made if the seller's free bond covers it, and it holds that much of the bond until its claim window ends or its claim resolves. A $10 bond can stand behind $10 of payments at a time. Buyers check before paying: GET /v1/bond/{id}?amount= answers covers.

It is also what stops a seller's second wallet from claiming the bond out from under real buyers: each payment's cover is its own. A seller can always reclaim cover nobody reserved, which is its own money.

What this cannot do

Stated here rather than buried, because a guarantee that oversells itself is worse than none.

  • It cannot tell whether sealed work was good, or sent. A seller that seals a record of every payment keeps its bond. The bond covers a seller that is paid and goes silent. A seller that lies has fixed its lie in time, before any dispute, which is the evidence an adjudicator needs.
  • Only payments made through THINKR are covered. The record is what makes a claim possible. Our first contract accepted claims on any reference, which let anyone claim on a payment that never happened; no bond was ever posted on it.
  • It is unaudited. Three independent reviews found no way to take or lock funds in this version, and each seller is capped at $250 so that any bug they missed is bounded. Bond only what you can afford to lose.
  • An outage longer than six hours can still cost a seller its seals for those hours, and a seller should seal before transferring its agent: afterwards only the new owner can.
