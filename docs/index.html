A seller posts money
before it can take yours.

x402 settles instantly and finally. There is no chargeback, no dispute, nobody to call. THINKR does not change that and does not want to. It adds one reading a buyer can take before it pays, and one thing a seller stands to lose if it does not deliver.

Not escrow

The buyer's payment is never held, delayed or routed through us. It settles over x402 exactly as it would otherwise. What sits in the contract is the seller's own money, posted in advance, covering everything it sells.

A plumber is bonded. You do not put his fee in escrow. You check his bond before you let him in the house.

The hour is the unit

Every hour a seller commits one merkle root of what it delivered in that hour. It has one further hour to publish it. After that the root is final, published or not.

  1. 00:00–01:00

    the seller delivers. Each delivery is a leaf: keccak256(ref, digest). The ref is what both sides call the transaction (an x402 payment, an action_ref, a receipt id); the digest is the seller's evidence in whatever format it already keeps.

  2. 01:00–02:00

    the window to commit that hour's root. Miss it and the hour has no root at all.

  3. from 02:00

    claims against that hour may be filed. The root is already fixed, so a seller can never see a claim and then write a root that answers it.

That ordering is the whole design. Everything else is bookkeeping around it.

Silence pays out

A buyer that paid and received nothing files a claim and stakes 2% of it. The seller has 48 hours to answer with a proof that the delivery is in the root it already committed. One call, and the claimant's stake goes to the seller.

No answer, and after 48 hours anyone can settle it. The claimant takes its stake back plus the claim, out of the bond.

The floor is a share, not a number

A claim must be at least 1% of the bond it is filed against. A $1 bond accepts 1 cent claims and a $10 bond 10 cent ones, the size agents actually pay today. A $5,000 bond requires $50 claims. Nothing has to be redeployed when payments grow.

It is also the defence against flooding, and a stronger one than a fee. Because every open claim reserves at least a hundredth of the bond, and reservations can never exceed the bond, a seller can face at most 100 open claims at once. The attack is bounded by arithmetic.

What this cannot do

Stated here rather than buried, because a guarantee that oversells itself is worse than none.

  • It cannot verify the payment happened. There is no on chain link from an x402 settlement to a claim that holds across facilitators. What makes a false claim unprofitable is the claimant's stake plus the seller's ability to kill it in one call. The alternative is routing payments through us, which is escrow, which breaks the thing x402 exists for.
  • A trade made late in a notice period is thinly covered. Notice is visible on chain the moment it is given. Read the bond before you pay.
  • It does not judge quality. A delivery that is in the root is discharged, whether or not it was any good. This answers "did anything arrive", not "was it right".
